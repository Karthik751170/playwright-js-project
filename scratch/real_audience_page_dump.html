Strategic Research Framework

Core Objective & Business Context

  • Goal: Evaluate consumer appeal, organoleptic expectations (sweetness, crunch, juiciness), packaging format preferences, and purchase intent for a new fresh apple variety in the Indian market.
  • Context: Fresh produce branding and premium fruit varieties (e.g., imported or proprietary domestic cultivars like Pink Lady, Fuji, or Royal Gala alternatives) are gaining traction in urban Indian retail. Launching a new variety requires understanding whether consumers perceive enough differentiation over standard Shimla/Kashmir apples to justify a premium price.
  • Decision enabled: Determine whether to proceed with commercial scaling, optimal shelf positioning (e.g., loose vs. clamshell/tray pack), pricing architecture per kg/pack, and core value proposition messaging.

Secondary Research Snapshot

  • Category Growth: India's fresh fruit retail market is rapidly modernizing, with apples representing one of the highest-value imported and domestic fruit categories in urban markets.
  • Consumer Behavior: According to industry trade insights, urban Indian consumers increasingly prioritize crisp texture, consistent sweetness, and visual appeal, often willing to pay a 20% to 40% premium for branded or imported apple varieties over unbranded local stock.
  • Channel Shift: Quick-commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) and modern trade channels have created dedicated demand for standardized, branded fruit packaging.

Research Type & Methodology

  • Study type: Concept Testing combined with Usage & Attitude (U&A) modules.
  • Why this approach: Single Monadic Concept Exposure allows respondents to evaluate the proposed new apple variety profile (visual presentation, descriptor, origin, crispness/flavor claims, and price point) without cross-variant contamination, providing clean top-2-box purchase intent benchmarks.
  • Alternatives considered: Sequential Monadic testing was considered if comparing multiple distinct varieties side-by-side, but Single Monadic is selected for a cleaner baseline read on the primary launch concept.
  • User control: If you prefer evaluating 2–3 candidate varieties in a comparative grid instead of testing a single launch profile, we can adjust to a Sequential Monadic design before brief generation.
  • Survey design logic: Screen for category buyers -> Measure current apple buying habits & benchmarks -> Present monadic product concept -> Evaluate standard KPIs (appeal, differentiation, purchase intent, value perception) -> Pricing tolerance (Van Westendorp PSM).
  • Quality controls: Speeding detection, straight-lining filters on imagery grids, and open-ended quality checks on diagnostic feedback.

Target Audience Profile

  • Who we are asking: Primary grocery decision-makers aged 22–55 in Tier 1 and Metro Indian cities who purchase fresh apples at least twice a month.
  • Why they matter: Regular apple shoppers have established price benchmarks and quality expectations, making their feedback representative of commercial launch adopters.
  • Screening criteria: Must buy fresh apples regularly; split across Modern Trade/Quick-Commerce buyers and traditional fruit vendor shoppers across NCCS A1–B1.

Key Information Areas & Data Architecture

  • Current Category Behavior: Purchase frequency, preferred origin/varieties (Kashmiri, Himachali, Washington), average monthly spend, and key decision drivers (crispness, sweetness, size, color) -> Uncovers baseline category expectations.
  • Concept Uniqueness & Appeal: Reaction to the new variety's descriptor, visual appeal, and positioning statement -> Identifies key perceptual hooks and differentiation from incumbent varieties.
  • Purchase Intent (Top-2-Box): 5-point purchase intent scale at target price point -> Provides go/no-go volume benchmark.
  • Price Sensitivity: Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (Too Cheap, Bargain, Expensive, Too Expensive) -> Establishes optimal price band per kg or per 4-pack.
  • Packaging & Channel Preference: Preference for loose vs tray/box packaging and preferred buying channel -> Guides supply chain and retail strategy.

Survey Flow & Question Design Approach

  • Phase 1 — Screening & Category Habits (3 questions): Confirm purchase frequency, decision role, and current apple varieties bought.
  • Phase 2 — Attribute Prioritization & Unmet Needs (3 questions): Rate importance of crunch, sweetness, shelf life, color, and organic certification.
  • Phase 3 — Monadic Concept Evaluation (5 questions): Present concept card (image + description + price per kg); measure overall appeal, believability, differentiation, and purchase intent.
  • Phase 4 — Pricing & Value Diagnostics (4 questions): Price perception battery and Van Westendorp pricing ladder.
  • Phase 5 — Channel & Packaging Preference (2 questions): Clamshell vs loose preferences and preferred buying channel (q-comm, supermarket, local vendor).
  • Estimated length: 17 questions (~8–10 minutes on mobile devices).

RESEARCH BRIEF

Background & Context

Fresh produce branding and premium fruit varieties (such as imported or proprietary domestic cultivars like Pink Lady, Fuji, or Royal Gala alternatives) are gaining rapid traction in urban Indian retail. As quick-commerce platforms (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) and modern trade channels expand, urban Indian consumers increasingly prioritize crisp texture, consistent sweetness, and visual appeal, often paying a 20% to 40% premium for standardized branded apples over unbranded local stock. Launching a new apple variety requires establishing whether consumers perceive enough sensory and quality differentiation over standard Shimla/Kashmir apples to justify a premium price point and determining the optimal packaging and positioning strategy.

Research Objectives

Based on your strategic requirements, here are the core business objectives of this study:

  • Strategic Objectives:

    • Strategic Objective: Evaluate the overall consumer appeal, organoleptic expectations, and purchase intent for a new premium apple variety in the urban Indian market.
    • Strategic Objective: Enable go/no-go commercial launch decisions, optimal price architecture (INR per kg/pack), and channel-specific packaging formats.

  • Tactical Objectives:

    • Objective: Benchmark baseline category purchasing habits, preferred origins (Kashmiri, Himachali, Washington), purchase frequency, and key decision drivers (crispness, sweetness, color, size).
    • Objective: Measure single-monadic concept performance (overall appeal, perceived crunch/flavor claims, visual appeal, and top-2-box purchase intent).
    • Objective: Determine price elasticity and optimal price points using the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM).
    • Objective: Identify channel-specific packaging format preferences (e.g., eco-friendly clamshell/tray pack vs. loose bulk).

Type of Study

We will conduct a Concept Testing combined with Usage & Attitude (U&A) study utilizing a Single Monadic Exposure for the concept card combined with a Van Westendorp pricing module.

Survey Design Approach (Marketing Research Methodology)

This research utilizes a Single Monadic Concept Exposure combined with a behavioral U&A module. Respondents evaluate a single proposed apple variety profile (visual presentation, descriptor, crispness/flavor claims, origin, and price anchor) in isolation. This eliminates cross-variant contamination and provides clean top-2-box purchase intent benchmarks against established produce standards.

Design Selection Logic:

  • Selected Design: Single Monadic Exposure (1 stimulus concept card tested per respondent).
  • Stimulus Count: 1 core launch concept card.
  • Why it fits: Provides an uncontaminated, reliable absolute measure of purchase intent and price acceptability for the proposed launch cultivar.
  • Alternatives Considered:
    1. Sequential Monadic: Considered if comparing 2-3 candidate apple varieties side-by-side, but rejected for this baseline launch evaluation to prevent respondent fatigue and contrast bias.
    2. Pure Monadic Split-Cell: Rejected as only 1 primary launch profile is being tested in this wave.
    3. Paired Comparison: Rejected because absolute volume diagnostics and price sensitivity curves are required rather than relative preference.

Survey Routing Logic:

  • Skip Logic: Bypasses non-buyer categories or non-decision makers.
  • Termination Logic: Screens out respondents who buy fresh apples less than twice a month or fall outside NCCS A1–B1.
  • Completion Logic: Gracefully completes after pricing diagnostics and packaging preferences.

Quality Controls:

  • Attention Checks: Embedded trap questions to filter out automated or speeder responses.
  • Speedster Detection: Flags completion times below 40% of the median.
  • Straight-lining Detection: Monitors low-variance grid responses on rating batteries.

You can request a different methodology before generating the survey.

Question Flow & Survey Phases

  • Phase 1 — Warm-Up & Category Screening: Qualify decision-makers purchasing fresh apples at least twice monthly in metro/tier-1 cities. Capture purchase frequency and preferred buying channels.
  • Phase 2 — Category Baseline & Attribute Importance: Evaluate current variety repertoire (Shimla, Kashmiri, Washington, Fuji) and rate importance of organoleptic attributes (crunch/crispness, sweetness, shelf life, color, organic status).
  • Phase 3 — Monadic Concept Exposure & Diagnostics: Present the new apple variety concept card (visual, descriptor, crunch claim, origin). Measure overall appeal, perceived differentiation, believability, and top-2-box purchase intent.
  • Phase 4 — Pricing Diagnostics (Van Westendorp PSM): Administer 4-question price sensitivity battery (Too Cheap, Cheap/Bargain, Expensive, Too Expensive) to establish optimal price band per kg/4-pack.
  • Phase 5 — Packaging & Channel Wrap-Up: Measure preference between clamshell/tray packaging vs. loose display and primary quick-commerce vs. supermarket shopping intent.

Key Data Points to Measure

Primary Metrics (KPIs):

  • Top-2-Box Purchase Intent (%) - enables volume benchmarking against fresh produce launch standards (>65% target)
  • Organoleptic Attribute Scores - enables evaluation of crispness, sweetness, and juiciness expectations
  • Van Westendorp Price Ladder (OPP & PME) - enables precise INR pricing architecture per kg and per 4-pack
  • Uniqueness & Differentiation Score - enables assessment of positioning distinctiveness over Shimla/Kashmiri incumbents

Supporting Metrics (Contextual Data):

  • Channel Preference - provides context for quick-commerce (Blinkit/Zepto) vs. modern trade distribution
  • Packaging Format Choice - guides unit packing decisions (4-pack clamshell vs loose)
  • Current Brand/Origin Repertoire - maps existing market share and substitution risk

Advanced Analytics We'll Apply:

  • Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Modeling to calculate the Point of Marginal Expensiveness (PME) and Optimal Price Point (OPP).
  • Cross-Tabulation to compare quick-commerce loyalists vs. traditional vendor shoppers.

Expected Insights & Outcomes

By the end of this study, you will possess the data to:

  • Validate: The core hypothesis that urban Indian consumers are willing to pay a 20% to 40% price premium over standard seasonal apples for guaranteed crunch and superior sweetness.
  • Decide: Whether to proceed with commercial scaling of the new variety and select the optimal shelf positioning format (e.g., 4-pack premium clamshell vs. loose retail display).
  • Strategize: Establish exact launch pricing (INR per kg/pack) and core value proposition messaging focusing on texture and sweetness.
  • Key hypotheses being tested:
    1. Guaranteed crispness and sweetness drive higher purchase intent than origin claims alone.
    2. Quick-commerce shoppers show significantly higher willingness to pay for clamshell-packaged premium apples.
  • KPIs to report: Top-2-Box Purchase Intent, Uniqueness Score, Optimal Price Point (OPP), Indifference Price Point (IPP), and Clamshell Packaging Preference %.

Methodology

We will deploy an 18-question, approximately 8 to 10-minute survey on the SuperJ mobile application and the web to 400 verified Indian respondents across target metro and Tier 1 cities over a 5-day fielding period.

Sample Size Rationale: A total sample size of n = 400 provides a margin of error of approximately +/- 4.9% at a 95% confidence level, delivering statistical reliability for monadic concept scoring and Van Westendorp pricing modeling.

Data Collection Approach: SuperJ mobile application and web panel with strict audience pre-targeting. Quality Controls: Speedster detection, straight-lining filters, and attention check verification.

Target Audience

Total Sample Size: 400 respondents

Geographic Focus: Metro & Tier 1 Cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Pune, Hyderabad, Kolkata)

Demographics:

  • Gender: 50% Male / 50% Female
  • Age Range: 18-24 (12.5%), 24-35 (43.75%), 35-45 (31.25%), 45-55 (12.5%)
  • Socioeconomic Class: NCCS A1, A2, B1, B2 (Urban household decision-makers)

Behavioral/Psychographic Filters:

  • Regular fresh apple buyers (purchasing fresh apples at least twice per month).
  • Primary or joint grocery decision-makers in the household.

Screening Criteria: Must buy fresh apples regularly and shop across Modern Trade, Quick-Commerce, or Traditional Fruit Vendors.
